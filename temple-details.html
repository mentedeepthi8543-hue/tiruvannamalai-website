📖 About Arunachaleswarar Temple

Arunachaleswarar Temple, also known as Annamalaiyar Temple, is one of the most important temples dedicated to Lord Shiva. It is located at the foot of the sacred Arunachala Hill in Tiruvannamalai, Tamil Nadu.

Lord Shiva is worshipped here as Arunachaleswarar or Annamalaiyar, while Goddess Parvati is worshipped as Unnamulai Amman. The temple and Arunachala Hill are deeply connected, and devotees consider the hill itself to be a divine manifestation of Lord Shiva.

The temple is an important centre for Shaiva worship, pilgrimage, meditation and Girivalam. Devotees from different parts of India and other countries visit Tiruvannamalai throughout the year.

The temple complex contains grand entrance towers, mandapams, shrines, sacred tanks, stone corridors, carved pillars and large courtyards used for daily worship and major festival processions.

Presiding Deity: Lord Arunachaleswarar

Goddess: Unnamulai Amman

Sacred Element: Fire or Agni

Important Pilgrimage: Girivalam around Arunachala Hill

Location: Tiruvannamalai, Tamil Nadu

⏰ Temple Timings

The temple generally remains open from early morning until night, with an afternoon interval. During weekends, Pournami, Karthigai Deepam and other major festival days, devotees should arrive early because the queues can become very long.

  • Temple Opening Time: 5:30 AM
  • Morning Darshan: 5:30 AM – 12:30 PM
  • Afternoon Interval: 12:30 PM – 3:30 PM
  • Evening Darshan: 3:30 PM – 9:30 PM
  • Temple Closing Time: 9:30 PM

Temple timings may change during special pujas, important festivals, maintenance work or crowd management arrangements. Visitors travelling from a long distance should confirm the current timing before starting their journey.

🎫 Darshan Information

Arunachaleswarar Temple welcomes thousands of devotees. Darshan is managed through designated queues to ensure safe and orderly movement inside the temple.

Waiting time depends on the day, crowd level, festival schedule and special rituals. Weekdays are usually less crowded than weekends, Pournami days, Karthigai Deepam and Mahashivaratri.

  • General Darshan: Available through the regular queue, subject to temple arrangements.
  • Special Darshan: Special-entry facilities may be available through authorised temple counters or the official booking system.
  • Visitor Assistance: Senior citizens and differently-abled devotees may approach temple staff for available help.
  • Recommended Arrival: Arrive early in the morning or before the evening crowd increases.
  • Footwear must be removed before entering the designated temple areas.
  • Follow the queue system and the instructions given by temple staff and security personnel.

👕 Temple Dress Code

Devotees should wear clean, respectful and modest clothing suitable for a place of worship. Traditional Indian clothing is preferred because it reflects the sacred atmosphere of the temple.

Recommended for Men

Dhoti, veshti, kurta, full-length trousers, simple shirts or other clean and modest clothing.

Recommended for Women

Saree, salwar kameez, churidar, kurta with full-length bottoms or other modest traditional clothing.

Clothing to Avoid

Avoid shorts, very short clothing, sleeveless or revealing outfits, beachwear and clothing with disrespectful words or images.

Footwear

Shoes and slippers should be deposited at the authorised footwear stand before entering the temple.

📜 Temple Rules

Temple rules protect the sanctity of the premises and help every devotee worship peacefully. Visitors should cooperate with priests, temple staff, volunteers, security personnel and police.

  • Maintain silence and avoid loud conversations inside worship areas.
  • Keep mobile phones on silent mode. Mobile-phone use may be restricted in some temple areas.
  • Photography and videography may be prohibited near the sanctum and inside restricted areas.
  • Follow the darshan queue and do not cross barricades or restricted entrances.
  • Do not litter. Use the available waste bins and help keep the temple premises clean.
  • Smoking, alcohol, tobacco and intoxicating substances are not permitted within the temple premises.
  • Respect temple customs, priests, rituals and other devotees.
  • Avoid carrying large bags. Security checks may be conducted at the temple entrances.

🛕 Rajagopuram

The Eastern Rajagopuram of Arunachaleswarar Temple is one of the tallest temple towers in South India, standing at approximately 217 feet with eleven magnificent tiers.

Built during the Vijayanagara period, the Rajagopuram showcases remarkable Dravidian architecture, intricate stone carvings and beautiful sculptures depicting various deities and mythological stories.

For devotees, entering through the Rajagopuram symbolises leaving worldly worries behind and stepping into a sacred path of devotion and inner peace.

🔥 Agni Lingam

Agni Lingam is one of the sacred Ashta Lingams located along the Girivalam path around Arunachala Hill. It represents the Fire Element among the Pancha Bhootas.

Devotees believe worshipping Agni Lingam removes negativity, strengthens courage, improves confidence and brings spiritual purification.

During Girivalam, thousands of pilgrims stop at Agni Lingam to offer prayers, light lamps and meditate before continuing their sacred walk.

📜 Temple History

Arunachaleswarar Temple has a long and important history connected with the growth of Shaivism in Tamil Nadu. The temple developed over many centuries through the contributions of several South Indian dynasties.

The Cholas, Pandyas, Hoysalas and Vijayanagara rulers supported the expansion, renovation and maintenance of the temple. Inscriptions found in the temple complex provide information about donations, land grants, festivals, rituals and administrative activities.

According to Hindu tradition, Lord Shiva appeared here as an endless column of fire before Lord Vishnu and Lord Brahma. This divine form is associated with Arunachala Hill, and the temple represents the sacred fire element at the foot of the hill.

Tamil Shaiva saints praised Arunachala in devotional hymns. Over time, Tiruvannamalai became an important centre for saints, scholars, pilgrims, yogis and spiritual seekers.

The temple complex gradually expanded to include magnificent gopurams, mandapams, shrines, stone corridors, sacred tanks, processional streets and large courtyards used during major festivals.

🔱 Spiritual Importance

Arunachaleswarar Temple is one of the Pancha Bhoota Sthalams, the five sacred Shiva temples associated with the five natural elements. Tiruvannamalai represents the element of fire.

Arunachala Hill itself is worshipped as a sacred form of Lord Shiva. Devotees believe that seeing the hill, worshipping at the temple and performing Girivalam bring spiritual purification and peace.

Girivalam is the sacred clockwise walk around Arunachala Hill. It is performed as an act of devotion, surrender, discipline and prayer. Many devotees perform Girivalam on Pournami days.

The spiritual atmosphere of the temple, hill and nearby ashrams attracts pilgrims, meditators and seekers from different parts of the world.

  • Represents the sacred fire element among the Pancha Bhoota Sthalams.
  • Arunachala Hill is revered as a manifestation of Lord Shiva.
  • Girivalam is performed for devotion, peace, purification and spiritual progress.
  • Tiruvannamalai is an important centre for Shaiva worship and meditation.
  • Devotees pray for wisdom, inner peace, courage and removal of negative thoughts.

🎉 Special Pujas & Festivals

Arunachaleswarar Temple celebrates many important festivals and special worship occasions throughout the year. Festival days attract a large number of devotees and include processions, rituals, chanting and special darshan arrangements.

Karthigai Deepam

The most famous festival of Tiruvannamalai. A sacred flame is lit on the top of Arunachala Hill, symbolising Lord Shiva as an infinite column of fire.

Pournami Girivalam

On every full-moon day, thousands of devotees walk around Arunachala Hill and visit the sacred Ashta Lingams.

Maha Shivaratri

Devotees observe fasting, chanting, abhishekam, prayer and night-long worship dedicated to Lord Shiva.

Brahmotsavam

Traditional temple processions are conducted using decorated temple vehicles, devotional music and special rituals.

Pradosham

A special Shiva worship period observed on the thirteenth lunar day with prayers and temple rituals.

Daily Abhishekam

Ritual bathing, decoration, lamp worship and offerings are performed according to the daily temple schedule.
